Group assignment: travelling tracks

Some tracks live on one playlist. Others get around: same track, five different playlists. A curation team wants to know whether the ones that get around are recognisably different. Three questions, answered as one notebook, plus a short video of your group talking through what you decided.

The three questions

  • What is one row, and how far does each track get? State the grain, count how many playlists each track appears on, and show the check that convinced you it counts what you think.
  • Bolt on the family label. Predict the row count, merge with validate= and indicator=, and deal with whatever the lookup table throws at you.
  • Do the tracks that get around look different? Compare the two groups with counts next to every mean, move the cut-off, check inside each family, one chart, and a short write-up.

Hand in

  • One notebook, named M1_assignment_<group>.ipynb, with group number and names in the first cell.
  • One video, 8 minutes and at most 10, where everyone in the group speaks. Screen recording with your voices over it is fine. No slides, no editing.
  • Restart & Run All has to produce the notebook you hand in, from a clean kernel.
  • Data read from the URLs in the brief. Do not attach copies of the CSV files.
  • A decision log: one line per question, what you decided and what it cost.

What we will notice

It is not graded, and we do read them. We will pick up on whether the row meaning is shown rather than asserted, whether you predicted the row count before merging, whether counts appear next to means, and whether the language stays on the right side of cause and effect. Chart styling, notebook length and whether your answer matches ours are not of interest.
